If you have spent any time in the world of tech or startups, you have 
likely come across the philosophy of Naval Ravikant. Famous for his “[How to Get Rich](https://nav.al/rich)” tweetstorm, Naval argues that in the age of infinite leverage, the only way to build true wealth is through **Specific Knowledge** (SK). You can read Naval’s original breakdown [here](https://nav.al/specific-knowledge).

## Why the Confusion?

Inoticed that people often struggle to grasp what Specific Knowledge 
actually is at first. I was in the same boat. I believe this confusion 
arises largely because Naval adds his own commentary and philosophical 
advice on top of the core economic concept.

**Fundamentally, Specific Knowledge is the subset of your personality that has economic value.**

**It is the combination of your skills + your experiences.**

For example, Naval points to Scott Adams (creator of Dilbert), whose Specific Knowledge is a unique stack:

> Humor/Conversation Skills + Artistic Skill + Corporate Experience
> 

This is why Specific Knowledge is unique to everyone (at least in theory).

I say “in theory” because, in reality, many people possess a Specific Knowledge stack that looks common, such as:

> Moderate Soft Skills + High School Education
> 

Or, consider a typical Computer Science graduate, whose stack often looks like:

> Full-Stack Skills + 3 Months of Internship Experience
> 

This explains why new grads and juniors often have a hard time getting a job. As Naval notes, *you are paid for your Specific Knowledge*.

And
 if your knowledge isn’t unique, means lots of supply (people have very 
similar SK with yours) for the demand (that job you applied) that simply
 means you won’t get paid highly even if you are to be chosen.

## Clarifying the Myths

There are two major misconceptions about SK that are worth clearing up:

**1. “You can’t be trained for it” doesn’t mean “don’t get a degree.”**
 When Naval says specific knowledge cannot be taught, he means no 
education system can strictly manufacture someone with Scott Adams’ 
exact, unique stack. However, you can (and should) add new skills and 
sub-skills to your stack through education by mixing them in your own 
unique way.

**2. “It feels like play” doesn’t ignore learning skills that doesnt feel like play.** Naval’s statement that “b*uilding specific knowledge will feel like play to you*”
 does not mean that skills unaligned with your natural curiosity don’t 
count toward your SK. It is simply a reminder and advice: your core 
obsession driving your career must be intrinsic. You cannot fake the 
genuine curiosity required to outperform others in the long run.

## Summary

To recap, here is how to look at it:

**The Core Concepts:**

- **SK is a stack:** It is your unique combination of skills + experiences.
- **SK is your value:** It is the thing you get paid for. If you make it unique, you escape competition.

**Naval’s Advices:**

- **Follow curiosity:** Build your SK stack around your natural interests. This is the only way to have fun (”feels like play”) while becoming irreplaceable.
- **Think in stacks, not titles:** Education alone usually only provides generic skills. Don’t optimize
your career for generic titles like “Consultant” or “Digital Marketer.”
Instead, optimize for your unique Specific Knowledge stack.
